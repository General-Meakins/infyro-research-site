Scorecard methodology

Each company is scored 0–4 on five factors. Two measure risk (higher = more exposed); three measure protection or upside (higher = more shielded, or more of a beneficiary). The Net AI Disruption Score averages each side to a common scale, then takes the difference: avg(Pricing exposure, Margin exposure) − avg(Moat durability, Demand tailwind, AI response). Positive = net disruption risk. Negative = net AI beneficiary. Range runs roughly −4 to +4.

FactorWhat it measuresDirection
Seat/license pricing exposureShare of revenue tied to per-seat or per-license pricing that AI agents could compress or let customers automate aroundRisk (0–4)
Margin exposure to AI/inference costsWhether embedding AI features pressures gross margin, versus the company being a supplier that benefits from others' AI capexRisk (0–4)
Data & workflow moat durabilityHow defensible the incumbent's proprietary data, integrations, and switching costs are against an AI-native challengerProtective (0–4)
AI-driven demand tailwindDirect revenue/volume upside from other companies' AI buildout — chips, power, cooling, automation, logisticsUpside (0–4)
Competitive AI responseHow credible and fast the company's own AI product strategy is at defending or growing shareProtective (0–4)

Scores are a first-pass, directional read based on thesis groupings and company notes, not yet a fully researched pass in the way the Tokenomics figures are sourced and cross-checked. Treat them as a starting hypothesis per company, to be refined with real diligence (earnings-call language, disclosed AI-related revenue, customer commentary) before drawing firm conclusions.
